# Iraqi ladder — script to A2

The sequence. "Next" always means the first unticked item in the lowest unfinished phase. Every form is Iraqi; Levantine appears only where it differs and is tagged (Lev.). No Modern Standard Arabic anywhere.

## Phase 0 — Script
Week one · ~20 hours

Done when: By the end you read any Iraqi word aloud, including your dad's messages and Arabizi. You won't understand most of it. Understanding is the next four phases.

### The system (tick as each becomes automatic, not as each is learned)
1. ا ب ت ث ن ي — the tooth family — one shape, dots decide
2. ا د ذ ر ز و — the six that never join forward
3. ج ح خ — one shape, three of the sounds already in your ear
4. س ش · ص ض — the long-tooth pair and the emphatic pair
5. ط ظ · ع غ · ف ق — emphatics, the ع family, and the one-dot-two-dot pair
6. ك ل م ه — the last four
7. Four positional forms — initial, medial, final, isolated — one shape squashed
8. ة tā' marbūṭa — the feminine ending, reads as a or at
9. Hamza — أ إ ء ؤ ئ — one sound, five seats
10. لا and ى — lām-alif and alif maqṣūra
11. Harakat — fatha, kasra, ḍamma, sukūn, shadda — mostly unwritten
12. Sun and moon letters — when al- swallows its l: ish-shams, il-gamar
13. Iraqi extras — چ (ch) گ (g) پ (p) ڤ (v)
14. Arabizi key — 2 3 5 6 7 8 9 — read it at speed
15. Tracing done — five hours across the week, then closed forever
16. Decoding at speed — a line of unknown text read aloud without stalling

## Phase 1 — Survival Iraqi
Months 1–3 · ~100 hours · Levantine book, Iraqi mouth

Done when: By the end you can run the greeting ritual, introduce yourself, talk about family and food, and follow the shape of a conversation — who's talking about what — even when the words are gone. Roughly 600 words.

### Grammar (31 points — the shared spine, in Iraqi forms)
1. The greeting ritual — the long one, learned whole as a script — and said to your dad that night
2. شلونك / شلونچ / شكو ماكو — the how-are-you set and its answers
3. Politeness formulas — shukran, 3afwan, allah ykhallīk, in shā' allāh, mā shā' allāh, yalla
4. آني إنت إنتي هو هي إحنا إنتو هُمّ — ani, inta, inti, huwa, hiya, i7na, intu, humma
5. No 'is' — nominal sentences: ani ṭālba — I (am) a student
6. Possessive suffixes — -i -ak -ich -a -ha -na -kum -hum: bēti, my house
7. ال al- — the article, and where it assimilates
8. Gender and agreement — ة marks feminine; adjective follows and agrees
9. هذا هاي ذولة — hādha, hāy, dhōla — this, this (f.), these
10. شنو منو وين شوكت ليش شلون بيش چم — the question words, Iraqi set
11. أكو / ماكو — aku, māku — there is, there isn't. Iraq's signature word
12. عند + suffix — 3indi, 3indak, 3indich — to have
13. ما + verb — negating verbs
14. مو — mū — not, for nouns and adjectives: mū zēn
15. Numbers 1–10, teens, tens — and the gender flip on 3–10
16. Prices — bēsh hādha? — and the answer at speed
17. Present (imperfect) — prefixes a- t- y- n-, and the suffixes
18. دا- da- — the Iraqi progressive: da-adrus, I'm studying
19. Past (perfect) — -it -t -ti -at -na -tu -aw
20. راح ra7 + verb — future
21. أريد arīd + verb — want, and want to
22. Imperative — rū7, ta3āl, gūl, isma3, tfaḍḍal
23. Object suffixes on verbs — shifta, I saw him; shiftich, I saw you (f.)
24. Prepositions — b-, 3ala, min, l-, wiyya, gabul, ba3ad
25. Time words — hassa, il-yōm, bāchir, il-bār7a, min zamān
26. Days and hours — the week, is-sā3a cham?
27. Root and pattern — recognition only: k-t-b → kitāb, kātib, maktab, maktūb
28. Plurals — sound (-īn, -āt) and broken — recognise, don't derive
29. كلش / هوايه / شوية — kulish, hwāya, shwayya — very, a lot, a little
30. زين / خوش — zēn, khōsh — good, fine, okay
31. Iraqi vowels — why kitāb comes out ktāb and bēt is never bayt

### Speaking topics (each one a set piece you can run cold)
1. The greeting ritual, all the way through — and the answers to each line
2. Introducing yourself — name, from Leeds, what you do, abūya 3irāqi
3. Family — the word for every relative, and which side they're on
4. How are you, and answering — shlōnak, shaku māku, il-7amdulillāh
5. Food and dinner — what's cooking, I'm hungry, it's delicious, more please
6. Numbers and prices — out loud, fast, no translating
7. Where things are — in the house, in the street
8. Days, times, when? — and 'not now, later'
9. The weather — 7ārr, bārid, and complaining about it
10. Thanks, please, sorry — and the replies, which are the harder half
11. I'm learning Arabic — and why — the sentence you'll say most
12. The shape of a phone call — who, what about, how it ended — without the words yet

## Phase 2 — Getting the gist
Months 3–5 · ~120 hours · Levantine ear opens

Done when: By the end you can say what a five-minute clip was about, follow your dad's calls from the next room, and tell someone what you did today. Roughly 1,500 words. This is where the finish-line skill — understanding and translating — actually arrives.

### Grammar (26 points — the irregular verbs and the connectors)
1. Hollow verbs — gāl/ygūl, rā7/yrū7, shāf/yshūf, chān/ykūn
2. Defective verbs — ija/yiji, 7ichā/y7ichi, nisa/yinsa
3. Doubled verbs — 7aṭṭ/y7uṭṭ
4. Hamzated verbs — akal/yākul, akhadh/yākhudh
5. Active participle as present — rāyi7, gā3id, 3ārif — going, staying, I know
6. Verb forms II III V VII VIII X — recognise by shape and meaning drift, produce the common ones
7. اللي illi — the relative: who, that, which
8. إذا / لو — idha, law — if real, if unreal
9. Comparatives — akbar, a7san, aṭwal — 'than' is min
10. Superlatives — il-akbar, a7san wā7id
11. Connectors — w, bas, li'an, 3alamūd, lamma, ba3dēn, awwal
12. چان chān + verb — used to, was doing
13. بعد / بعد ما / خلاص — still, not yet, done
14. لازم lāzim — must, have to
15. أگدر agdar — can, be able
16. يمكن / بلكي — yimkin, balki — maybe
17. ممنوع / ما يصير — not allowed, can't be done
18. Dual — -ēn: yōmēn, two days
19. Collective and unit nouns — tuffā7 the apples, tuffā7a one apple
20. Question tags — ṣa7? mū?
21. Vocative — yā + name; yābā, yumma
22. Time expressions — gabul sana, ba3ad shahar, min zamān, hassa hassa
23. Levantine ear — b- prefix, 3am, biddi, hallaq, kīf, shū — recognise all of it
24. Egyptian ear — the dialect of half the media: the ten basics, recognition only
25. Dialect ID — Iraqi, Levantine, Egyptian, Gulf — tell them apart in a sentence
26. Iraqi rhythm — stress and dropped vowels, learned by shadowing your dad's recordings

### Speaking topics (the level where you stop reciting)
1. Your day, in order — then yesterday, then tomorrow
2. Describing a person — looks first, then character
3. Feeling ill — symptoms, the pharmacy, I'm tired
4. On the phone — the opening, who's this, I'll call back
5. A shop or market — asking, a little bargaining, paying
6. Directions — asking, and the harder part: following
7. Invitations — inviting, accepting, and the polite-refusal ritual
8. What you're studying and why — Korean and Arabic both, and the reaction
9. Your work — translating, editing videos — in words he'd use
10. Plans for the week — ra7 and lāzim, doing their jobs
11. Retelling a clip — 'it was about…' — one minute, in Iraqi
12. Telling your dad what you did today — in his language, at dinner

## Phase 3 — Small talk and translate
Months 5–7 · ~130 hours · the finish line

Done when: By the end you can hold small talk for ten minutes, translate a family conversation live for someone, and retell a story your dad told you back to him. Roughly 2,500 words. This is A2 with your particular shape: comprehension deep, speaking sufficient.

### Grammar (16 points — reported speech and the feeling-layer)
1. Reported speech — gāl inna…, gālat li…
2. Indirect questions — mā adri idha / shinu / wēn
3. Purpose — 7atta, 3ashān, 3alamūd
4. Passive patterns — in-, it- — inkisar, itbā3: recognition
5. Verbal nouns — dirāsa, shughul, akil — the maṣdar
6. Conditionals, extended — idha … ra7, law … chān
7. Each other, myself — ba3aḍ, nafsi
8. Exceptions — illa, ghēr, bas
9. Emphasis and oaths — walla, w-allāhi, 3ala rāsi
10. Discourse markers — ya3ni, khalāṣ, yalla, ṭayyib, zēn, hā — the words that run the conversation
11. Softeners — shwayya, ya3ni, mū hīchi?
12. Endearments and family register — 7abībi, 7abībti, 3ayni, rū7i, yābā, yumma
13. Sympathising — mā 3alēk, allāh ysā3dak, 3ala kēfak
14. Reproach and reminding — the Iraqi versions of я же говорила — you'll collect these from him
15. Levantine production, optional — the swap list, so you can answer a Jordanian in their own words if you ever want to
16. Code-switching — when Iraqis drop English or Standard into speech, and what it signals

### Speaking topics (the things that are actually yours)
1. Retelling a story your dad told — in Iraqi, back to him, and getting corrected
2. Opinions — agreeing, disagreeing gently, changing your mind
3. Something from the news — the gist, in your own words
4. The UK and Iraq — comparing; what surprised him, what would surprise you
5. Translating a conversation live — Arabic to English for someone in the room
6. Five small-talk set pieces — each runs two minutes cold
7. Life story, short version — past tense, ten sentences
8. Asking your dad about Iraq — where he grew up, what it was like
9. The conversation you want to have — you know which one

## Phase 4 — Proof
Running alongside Phase 3 · evidence, not study

Done when: No new grammar. Each item is a thing you either did or didn't do. Tick all of them and Arabic is done — it drops to twenty minutes a day of upkeep and Korean gets the room.

### Milestones (the finish line, itemised)
1. Read your dad's messages — without transliteration, without stalling
2. Read Arabizi at speed — comments, captions, whatever
3. A five-minute Iraqi clip — and say what it was about
4. A five-minute Levantine clip — the same
5. Translate a family conversation — live, for someone who doesn't speak it
6. Ten minutes with your dad — no English, no help
7. A phone call from the next room — the gist, without seeing anyone
8. Two minutes with an Iraqi stranger — small talk, and they don't switch to English
9. Name the dialect in ten seconds — Iraqi, Levantine, Egyptian, Gulf
10. Order and pay in a Jordanian restaurant — in Arabic, and get what you ordered
11. Retell one of his stories — to someone else, in Iraqi
12. Say the thing — to him, in his language

## Script week (3 h a day)

- **Day 1 — The tooth family and the six loners** (3 h): ا ب ت ث ن ي — one shape, dots decide. Then ا د ذ ر ز و — the six letters that never join forward, which is what makes word boundaries visible. Four positional forms, taught as one shape squashed. One hour tracing.
- **Day 2 — ج ح خ · س ش · ص ض** (3 h): Three shapes. ح and خ are sounds your dad makes forty times a day — say them while you write them. One hour tracing.
- **Day 3 — ط ظ · ع غ · ف ق** (3 h): The emphatics and the one that scares everyone else (ع) and won't scare you. ف and ق are one shape, one dot versus two. One hour tracing.
- **Day 4 — ك ل م ه · ة · لا · ى** (3 h): The last four, plus tā' marbūṭa (the feminine ending), lām-alif, and alif maqṣūra. By tonight you can decode any Iraqi word. One hour tracing.
- **Day 5 — Vowels, hamza, sun and moon, Iraqi extras** (3 h): Fatha, kasra, ḍamma, sukūn, shadda — mostly unwritten, so you learn to read without them. Hamza's five seats (أ إ ء ؤ ئ), one sound. Sun letters swallow the l of al-. Then چ گ پ ڤ, the letters Iraqi adds. Last hour of tracing, ever.
- **Day 6–7 — Decode only** (2 h each): Your dad's messages, shop signs, names, song titles, YouTube comments. Read aloud, out loud, without needing to understand. Plus the Arabizi key — twenty minutes and you can read half the Arab internet.

## The daily block (2 h, from week two)

- **20 min — Anki** (any time): Reviews first. Cards are sentences from your dad and from clips, in Arabic script with Arabizi on the back. Fifteen new a day, maximum.
- **25 min — Grammar + ten sentences out loud** (15:00–19:00): One point from the ladder, learned from the Levantine book, produced in Iraqi. Say every sentence, don't just write it — writing is banned after week one.
- **25 min — Listening** (15:00–19:00): One clip, 2–5 minutes, twice. First pass cold; second pass with a transcript, subtitles, or your dad. Write down what it was about in one English sentence — that's your finish-line skill practised daily.
- **20 min — Your dad** (evenings): He speaks to you in Arabic. You ask shinu hādha? at dinner. One phrase a day asked for deliberately, recorded on your phone, repeated back until he nods.
- **20 min — Reading in the wild** (anywhere): His messages, comments, captions, lyrics — script and Arabizi both. Not books. Dialect isn't in books.
- **10 min — Decoding drill** (anywhere): Read Arabic aloud without understanding it. Speed is the goal. This is the thing that makes the script automatic and it's the first thing people skip.

Floor day = Anki reviews + five sentences said out loud. Counts as complete.

Transliteration key (Arabizi): 2 = ء, 3 = ع, 5/kh = خ, 6 = ط, 7 = ح, 9 = ص, gh = غ, q = ق, g = گ, ch = چ. Long vowels ā ī ū / ē ō.
