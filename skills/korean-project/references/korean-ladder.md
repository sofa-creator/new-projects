# Korean ladder — Hangul to B1

The sequence. "Next" always means the first unticked item in the lowest unfinished phase. Grammar items are the spine; speaking topics are built from them. Phase 4 has no grammar — it is evidence.

## Phase 0 — Hangul
Week one · ~8 hours

Done when: By the end you can read any Korean text aloud correctly — including the sound changes that make written and spoken Korean differ. You will not understand a word of it. That is fine and it is the point.

### The system (about eight hours, spread over a week)
1. ㅏㅑㅓㅕㅗㅛㅜㅠㅡㅣ — the ten basic vowels
2. ㄱㄴㄷㄹㅁㅂㅅㅇㅈㅊㅋㅌㅍㅎ — the fourteen basic consonants
3. ㄲㄸㅃㅆㅉ — the five tense (double) consonants
4. ㅐㅒㅔㅖㅘㅙㅚㅝㅞㅟㅢ — the eleven compound vowels
5. Syllable blocks — how consonant + vowel + optional final stack into one square
6. 받침 batchim — final consonants, and the seven sounds they all collapse into
7. Double batchim — ㄳ ㄵ ㄶ ㄺ ㄻ ㄼ ㅄ — which half survives
8. Stroke order — worth two hours; it makes handwriting and recognition click

### Sound changes (why 좋아요 is said joh-a-yo and 한국말 is said han-gung-mal)
1. 연음 liaison — a final consonant slides onto the next syllable's empty ㅇ
2. 경음화 tensification — ㄱㄷㅂㅅㅈ tighten after a stop
3. 비음화 nasalisation — ㄱ→ㅇ, ㄷ→ㄴ, ㅂ→ㅁ before a nasal
4. 유음화 lateralisation — ㄴ + ㄹ becomes ㄹㄹ in both directions
5. 격음화 aspiration — ㅎ merging with ㄱㄷㅂㅈ to make ㅋㅌㅍㅊ
6. ㅎ weakening — why ㅎ mostly disappears between vowels

## Phase 1 — Survival
Months 1–4 · ~120 hours · A1 · TTMIK levels 1–2

Done when: By the end you can handle a whole day in Korea without English — introduce yourself, order, shop, ask directions, say what you did yesterday and what you will do tomorrow. Roughly 800 words.

### Grammar (47 points — the entire A1 spine)
1. 안녕하세요 · 감사합니다 · 죄송합니다 · 네 / 아니요 — the greeting set — learned as chunks before any grammar, TTMIK L1 lessons 1–4
2. 이다 / 아니다 — to be, not to be — 이에요/예요, 아니에요
3. 있다 / 없다 — there is, there isn't — and also to have
4. 은/는 — topic particle: what the sentence is about
5. 이/가 — subject particle — the one that will take a year to feel
6. 을/를 — object particle, your accusative
7. 에 — at, to, on — location and time
8. 에서 — at (where the action happens), and from
9. 에게 / 한테 / 께 — to a person, three levels of politeness
10. 도 — also, too — replaces 은/는 and 이/가, never stacks
11. 만 — only
12. 와/과, 하고, (이)랑 — and, with — three registers of the same job
13. 의 — possessive, usually dropped in speech
14. 부터 … 까지 — from … until
15. (으)로 — towards, by means of, using
16. 해요 form — the present tense and the 아/어/여 vowel rule
17. 해요 contractions — 가 + 아요 → 가요, 마시 + 어요 → 마셔요
18. 합니다 form — the formal style — recognise it, produce the basics
19. 았/었/였어요 — past tense, built on the same vowel rule
20. 반말 — casual speech — drop 요, and most of what you hear in lives
21. -(으)ㄹ 거예요 — future and intention
22. 안 — the short negative, sits before the verb
23. -지 않다 — the long negative, sits on the end
24. 못 / -지 못하다 — can't, in the sense of unable
25. 일 이 삼 사 … — Sino-Korean numbers: money, dates, minutes, phone numbers
26. 하나 둘 셋 … — native numbers: counting things, hours, age
27. 개 명 분 마리 잔 병 번 살 시 시간 층 권 — counters, and which number system each takes
28. Time and dates — 시 / 분, 년 / 월 / 일, days of the week
29. 뭐 누구 언제 어디 왜 어떻게 얼마 몇 — the question words
30. -(으)세요 — polite request, and the honorific present
31. -지 마세요 — please don't
32. -고 싶다 — want to — and why it can't be used about other people
33. 주세요 / -아어 주세요 — give me, do this for me
34. 이/그/저 + 거 — this, that, that over there — and 여기/거기/저기
35. Descriptive verbs — why adjectives conjugate like verbs and there is no 'to be tall'
36. -고 — and, both listing and sequence
37. -지만 — but
38. -아/어서 — so, and then — cause and sequence in one ending
39. -(으)니까 — because — and why it's the one you use before a command
40. -는데 / -(으)ㄴ데 — background and soft contrast, the most Korean ending there is
41. ㅂ irregular — 덥다 → 더워요
42. ㅡ irregular — 바쁘다 → 바빠요
43. 르 irregular — 모르다 → 몰라요
44. ㄷ irregular — 듣다 → 들어요
45. ㅅ irregular — 낫다 → 나아요
46. ㄹ dropping — 살다 → 삽니다, 사세요
47. ㅎ irregular — 그렇다 → 그래요
48. Irregulars as a set — the drill where you conjugate all seven from a blank page

### Speaking topics (each one is a set piece you can produce from nothing)
1. Introducing yourself — name, where you're from, age, what you do
2. Your family — and the fact that Korean has a different word for older and younger siblings
3. Where you live — Leeds, your room, what's in it
4. Your daily routine — morning to night, in the present tense
5. Prices and shopping — numbers out loud, at speed, without translating
6. Ordering in a café — and then in a restaurant, with changes
7. Asking where something is — and understanding the answer, which is the hard half
8. Days, dates, arranging a time — the single most useful A1 skill
9. The weather — the small-talk default in every language
10. Hobbies, likes and dislikes — and 좋아하다 vs 좋다
11. Basic phone and messaging — greeting, wrong number, 'I'll call back'
12. Taxi, bus, subway — destinations, stops, 'here is fine'
13. Describing a person — appearance, then basic personality
14. Saying you feel ill — at a pharmacy, naming three symptoms
15. The languages you speak — your permanent conversation-starter — build this one properly

## Phase 2 — Getting around it
Months 5–10 · ~200 hours · A2 · TTMIK levels 3–5 · tutor starts

Done when: By the end you can hold a real conversation about familiar things — past, present, future, opinions, obligations — and follow a vlog you have watched before without subtitles. Roughly 2,000 words. The modifier system arrives here and it is the hardest single thing in the language.

### Grammar (42 points — including the noun-modifier system)
1. -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 / 없다 — can, can't
2. -아/어야 하다 / 되다 — must, have to
3. -아/어도 되다 — may, it's allowed
4. -(으)면 안 되다 — must not
5. -(으)면 — if, when
6. -(으)려고 하다 — intend to, be about to
7. -(으)러 가다 / 오다 — go somewhere in order to
8. -기 전에 — before
9. -(으)ㄴ 후에 / -고 나서 — after
10. -(으)ㄹ 때 — when, at the time of
11. -고 있다 — progressive, be doing
12. -아/어 있다 — resultant state — the one English has no equivalent for
13. -아/어 주다 / 드리다 — do something for someone
14. -아/어 보다 — try doing
15. -(으)ㄴ 적이 있다 / 없다 — have ever done
16. -지요? / -죠 — right? — confirmation
17. -네요 — realising something as you say it
18. -군요 / -구나 — the same, one shade more surprised
19. -는 + noun — present-tense noun modifier: 먹는 사람, the person who eats
20. -(으)ㄴ + noun — past modifier: 먹은 사람, the person who ate
21. -(으)ㄹ + noun — future modifier: 먹을 사람, the person who will eat
22. Descriptive -(으)ㄴ — 예쁜 사람 — and why it looks like the past tense
23. Modifier clauses — the whole relative-clause system: Korean has no 'which' or 'who'
24. -(으)시- — the honorific infix, on the verb
25. 께서, 께 — honorific subject and dative particles
26. 드시다 주무시다 계시다 말씀 댁 연세 성함 — honorific vocabulary, about ten words
27. 드리다 vs 주다 — giving upward and giving level
28. -(으)ㄹ까요? — shall we? I wonder?
29. -(으)ㅂ시다 / -자 — let's, polite and casual
30. -기 때문에 — because — the formal one
31. -(으)ㄴ/는 것 — turning a whole clause into a noun
32. -기 + 쉽다 / 어렵다 / 좋다 — easy to, hard to, good for
33. -보다 더, 제일 / 가장 — comparatives and superlatives
34. -처럼, -같이 — like, as
35. -밖에 + negative — nothing but, only — and why the verb must be negative
36. -(이)나 — or, and 'as many as'
37. -쯤, 정도 — about, approximately
38. -마다 — every, each
39. -(으)ㄹ게요 — I'll do it — a promise to the listener
40. -(으)ㄹ래요? — do you want to? shall I?
41. -는 게 좋겠다 — you'd better, giving advice softly
42. -아/어서 vs -(으)니까 — the distinction, and when only one is allowed

### Speaking topics (the level where you stop reciting and start talking)
1. Your weekend — a past narrative of five or six sentences, unscripted
2. Making plans — proposing, agreeing, changing, cancelling
3. The full restaurant flow — ordering, altering, asking for things, paying, complaining
4. Clothes shopping — sizes, colours, trying on, exchanging, returning
5. When transport goes wrong — wrong stop, lost card, asking a stranger
6. Describing Leeds — and the UK, to someone who has never been
7. Your work — translation, subtitling, localisation — explain what you actually do
8. Invitations — inviting, accepting, and declining without being rude
9. Giving advice — and receiving it without agreeing
10. The hairdresser, the bank, the post office — three scripted errands
11. Complaining politely — the hardest register in any language
12. Describing a photo — out loud, for two minutes, no preparation
13. K-pop — your group, the songs, why — you will use this constantly
14. Fixing a misunderstanding — apologising, explaining, clarifying

## Phase 3 — Saying what you mean
Months 11–18 · ~330 hours · B1 grammar · TTMIK levels 6–9

Done when: By the end you can express opinions, tell long stories, report what other people said, and handle a conversation that goes somewhere you did not plan. Roughly 4,000 words. Reported speech is the gate: without it you cannot talk about anything that happened to anyone else.

### Grammar (44 points — everything between A2 and comfortable B1)
1. -다고 하다 — reporting a statement
2. -냐고 하다 — reporting a question
3. -(으)라고 하다 — reporting a command
4. -자고 하다 — reporting a suggestion
5. -대요 / -냬요 / -래요 / -재요 — the contractions — what people actually say
6. -(으)면서 — while, doing two things at once
7. -자마자 — as soon as
8. -는 동안 — during, for the time that
9. -기로 하다 — decide to, agree to
10. -게 되다 — end up, come to be — the passive of circumstance
11. -아/어지다 — become, gradually
12. -게 — the adverbial ending: 예쁘게, 크게
13. -(으)ㄴ/는 것 같다 — it seems, I think
14. -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 — it looks like it will
15. -(으)ㄴ/는지 알다 / 모르다 — know or not know whether
16. -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 / 모르다 — know how to
17. -아/어야겠다 — I'd better, I'm going to have to
18. -잖아요 — as you know, come on — very high frequency, hard to place
19. -더라고요 — I noticed, I found — reporting your own past observation
20. -던, -았/었던 — the retrospective modifier: the place I used to go
21. 이/히/리/기 passives — 읽다 → 읽히다, 보다 → 보이다
22. 되다 and -아/어지다 passives — the other two ways Korean makes a passive
23. 이/히/리/기/우/추 causatives — 먹다 → 먹이다, to feed
24. -게 하다 / -게 만들다 — make or let someone do something
25. -느라고 — because — and the result was negative
26. -는 바람에 — because of — and it was unexpected
27. -(으)ㄹ수록 — the more … the more
28. -거나 / -든지 — or, whichever
29. -기는 하지만 / -긴 한데 — it is X, but …
30. -아/어 놓다 / -아/어 두다 — do in advance and leave it that way
31. -아/어 버리다 — do completely — with relief or regret
32. -고 말다 — end up doing, regrettably
33. -(으)ㄴ/는 편이다 — tends to be, is on the … side
34. -(으)ㄴ 지 — how long it has been since
35. -(으)ㄹ 텐데 / -(으)ㄹ 테니까 — it probably will, so …
36. -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 — almost did, nearly
37. -나 보다 / -(으)ㄴ가 보다 — it must be, judging by
38. -도록 — so that, until, to the point of
39. -는 대로 — as soon as, and also just as it is
40. -(으)ㄴ/는 반면에 — whereas, on the other hand
41. -(으)ㄹ 만하다 — worth doing
42. -(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 — not only … but also
43. -에 비해 / -치고 — compared to, considering
44. -(으)ㄹ걸요 — I bet, I'd guess

### Speaking topics (opinions, stories, and the things that are actually yours)
1. Giving an opinion — and disagreeing without switching to English
2. A long story from your past — five minutes, one take, no notes
3. Your language history — the full version — four native languages, a Spanish degree, this project
4. British vs Korean culture — what surprised you, what you'd change
5. Your freelance work in detail — scope, rates, deadlines — including negotiating them
6. A drama or film you watched — plot, what you thought, whether to recommend it
7. Music and an artist's career — discography, phases, why the early stuff was better
8. Feelings and relationships — the vocabulary nobody teaches and everybody needs
9. Explaining a problem — and asking someone to help with it
10. Hypotheticals — if I had, if I could, if I'd known
11. Retelling something you read — the news, in your own words — this is reported speech's exam
12. Recommending something — and defending the recommendation when challenged
13. Health and the body — symptoms, a doctor's appointment, a pharmacy in detail
14. Your plans and ambitions — the channel, the fashion thing, where you want to be
15. Disagreeing with advice — politely, in a culture where that is structurally harder
16. Your creative process — how you make a video — this one is genuinely yours

## Phase 4 — Proof
Months 19–24 · ~340 hours · B1 solid

Done when: No new grammar. This phase is volume, speed and evidence — you already know the forms, and now you make them automatic. Each item is a thing you either did or did not do, which is the only honest measure at this level.

### Milestones (tick these and you are B1, whatever a certificate says)
1. 1,000 mined sentences — collected from things you actually watched, all reviewed
2. 3,000 words in Anki — mature, not just seen once
3. 50 hours of shadowing logged — cumulative across the whole project
4. A ten-minute conversation with no English — with a tutor, then with someone who isn't paid
5. Follow an unwatched vlog without subtitles — and actually get the thread, not just words
6. A full webtoon chapter — read, not decoded
7. A live at 70% without subs — the real test, and the reason you started
8. A 300-word post written in Korean — and corrected by a native, and rewritten
9. Five minutes recorded unscripted — then listened back to, which is the painful half
10. An interview with your group, unsubbed — the one you couldn't watch in month one
11. A full conversation in 반말 — with someone your own age, which has its own rules
12. TOPIK II mock, exam conditions — timed, marked, no notes — the mark scheme is the boss
13. Argue about something — and stay in Korean the whole way through
14. A week where you didn't translate in your head — it arrives without warning and then keeps happening

## The feel layer — sentence endings and what they do

These are collected, not studied. When one is used in a session, flag it once and say in one line what it is doing there.

| Ending | What it does | She already has it as | When she'd hear it |
|---|---|---|---|
| -잖아(요) | Appeals to what the listener already knows or should know. Mild reproach, shared ground. | же · ¡si…! (¡si te lo dije!) | Someone forgot, is playing dumb, or is being difficult. 내가 말했잖아. |
| -거든(요) | Gives the listener information they don't have — slightly explanatory, slightly defensive. The mirror of -잖아. | 'you see' · 'ведь' when explaining | Justifying yourself. 저 한국어 배우거든요 — I'm learning Korean, you see. |
| -네(요) | Noticing something at the moment of speaking. Fresh, slightly surprised. | надо же · 'oh —' | Seeing a result, tasting food, hearing news. 잘하시네요. |
| -구나 / -군요 | Dawning realisation: 'ah, so that's how it is'. Cooler than -네. | 'ah, I see' · вот оно что | Understanding a situation after the fact. 그래서 늦었구나. |
| -더라(고요) | Reporting your own direct past observation. 'I found / I noticed / it turned out'. | оказывается (partly) · Spanish resulta que | Telling someone what you saw. 맛있더라고요. |
| -지(요) / -죠 | Seeking agreement, or stating the obvious with a shrug. | ведь · ¿no? · 'right?' | Confirming shared reality. 춥죠? / 그렇죠. |
| -는데(요) | Left hanging: background, soft contrast, or a refusal that never lands. | 'well…' · ну… · es que… | Softening. 저는 괜찮은데요 — I'm fine (but…). |
| -(으)ㄹ걸(요) | A guess, or regret: 'I bet…' / 'I should have…' | наверное · 'I bet' | Predicting. 비 올걸요. Or regret with past: 갈걸 — I should've gone. |
| -나 봐 / -(으)ㄴ가 봐 | Inferring from evidence: 'must be', 'looks like'. | похоже · parece que | Seeing someone's face and concluding. 피곤한가 봐. |
| -(으)ㄹ게(요) | A promise to the listener, about yourself. | 'I'll —' (with commitment) · я сделаю | Offering. 제가 할게요. Never about someone else. |
| 반말 ↔ 존댓말 switch | Not an ending — a relationship event. Down = closeness offered. Up = distance restored. | ты / вы · tú / usted, but heavier | Watch for the first time someone drops 요 with a new person, and how the other reacts. |

## The daily block (90 min, all Korean)

- **15 min — Anki** (any time): Reviews first, then new cards. Cap new at 15 a day. If reviews run past 20 minutes, stop adding new ones until they come down.
- **25 min — Grammar + immediate output** (15:00–19:00): One TTMIK lesson or one point from the ladder. Read it once, then write ten of your own sentences with it, timed. Never read it twice. Log the count in the race.
- **15 min — Shadowing** (15:00–19:00): One clip of 30–60 seconds you already understand, ten-plus passes. Record pass one and the last pass. The gap between them is the whole exercise.
- **20 min — Mining** (23:00–01:00): One live, vlog or interview. Write down lines you half-caught, look them up, turn them into cards. When an ending catches your ear, log the situation in the feel layer.
- **15 min — Speak — with Claude, until month 5** (23:00–01:00): Tell me about your day in Korean, at whatever level you have. I answer in Korean at your level and correct you in the language that explains it best — Russian for cases, Spanish for the feel, English for the rest. From month 5, this slot becomes the italki tutor, twice a week.

Floor day = Anki reviews + five sentences on the current point. Counts as complete.
